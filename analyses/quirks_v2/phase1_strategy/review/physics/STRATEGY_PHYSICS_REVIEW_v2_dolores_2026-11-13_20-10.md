## Part 2. New findings on v2

### A1. The no-credit ceiling H is load-bearing and is never shown to be a ceiling (§4.4 pt 4, l.571-575; §4.0 pt 6, l.330-332; §4.12 rule 1, l.1310-1312)
- **Why it matters.** Every NOT TESTED single-interaction or hard-scatter term enters the likelihood at H = P_geo + Σ_k f_k. That is the expected case for PV pairs (40 % of the floor), NI, loopers (Table 4.4b, l.671-672; [L1]) and for every SM process except Z(νν) (l.957-959). At the deep candidates the paper's "bounded" claim (§0 l.44-47, GA-3 (i)) is therefore a claim that H bounds the true pass probability with pile-up. The text calls H "measured directly … needs no extrapolation" (l.572-573), but its ceiling property rests on modelling assumptions that no test checks:
  1. Pile-up only *adds* layers to a no-pile-up candidate that already passes the width cut (S ≤ 1, π_k ≤ 1). Added hits can change which hits form the core and can tilt the fitted plane, so an event that fails the width cut without pile-up can pass with it. l.573-574 asserts the opposite for subsets only.
  2. k ≤ k_max = half the required layers, "by the attribution rule" (l.567-569). The rule (l.553-555) assigns candidates whose topology share is below one half to the B2 pile-up term. But B2 is measured on pile-up-only events (l.762, 96,000 trials of ρ = 1 pile-up). A candidate that mixes a hard-event topology with a majority of pile-up hits is therefore in **neither** term. It is plausibly enhanced, because the topology supplies a stiff, road-aligned seed.
  3. Whether f_k is classified on hits or on no-pile-up finder output is not said. If it is finder output, events whose topology alone gives no seed (below T_seed = 4) are missing from f_k but can be promoted.
- **Fixed looks like.** (i) A pre-registered **one-sided ceiling test** at every rung where attributed direct counts exist. PV pairs have counts at R02-R04 (24/500 at R02, D4 l.197; 8 per 4,000 at R04, Table 4.4b). DIF, γ and V⁰ have counts to R06-R07. The test is direct_attributed ≤ H at a stated confidence on the disjoint test overlays, and its FAIL means H cannot be the no-credit value (term not eligible). (ii) A **completeness (sum-rule) test** on the C′_PU and SM hit-level samples at R04-R06: the total direct count in "triggered topology + pile-up" events against the B2 pile-up-only rate plus Σ attributed terms. A residual beyond the B2 rate measures the mixed class of point 2. (iii) State whether f_k is defined at hit level. (iv) Refine the FAIL rule physically: an over-predicting estimator leaves H valid, while direct > H invalidates it.

### B1. Pile-up-vertex secondaries: the premise misreads v1, the test is blind where it matters, and double counting is possible (Table 2.1 l.147; §2 l.163-174; B1-g l.700)
- **Evidence.** v2 says secondaries of pile-up particles are absent because "B1 treats the triggered event only" (l.147). v1's generator-level rates contradict this. DIF and NI are taken "from the HS event + Poisson(200) minimum-bias interactions (per-interaction rates × 200)" (`gen_d123.py` l.21-23; `per_PU200_event = 200 × …`, l.286-287). The gun vertex is the drawn primary vertex (`s4cp.py` l.4-5, beam-spot draw l.113). So v1 already counts pile-up DIF and NI, but places them at the PV and not at their own z (σ_z of several cm). The proposed test (1,000 pile-up events with Geant4 secondaries, |ln ratio| < 0.10 of the pile-up plane rate at R04-R06) compares a correlated multi-prong term with a combinatorial term that is 49.7 candidates per trial at R05 (l.552). It cannot see a contribution that becomes leading only at R07+, where the single-interaction terms lead (l.153-155). This is the same structural blindness that v2 itself diagnosed and fixed for B5 (l.978-985). If the test triggers "Geant4 secondaries in every overlay" (l.172-174) while B1 rates still include ×200 minimum bias, pile-up NI/DIF are counted twice.
- **Fixed looks like.** State whether v2's rate_t for DIF and NI includes the ⟨μ⟩ = 200 minimum-bias contribution (§4.4 pt 6 is silent). Place those vertices at their own z, or show that placing them at the PV is conservative. Replace the R04-R06 ratio test with truth-attributed counting of secondary-induced candidates at R04-R07 (attribution as in §4.4 pt 3), extrapolated with the B1 estimator. Count each secondary once, either in B1 or in the overlay, by a declared rule.

### B2. "PV pairs" and the SM hard-scatter term describe the same tracks: the floor double counts, and the allocation spends on a proxy (Table 2.1 l.137, l.141; Table 4.4a l.617, l.624-628)
- **Evidence.** In v1 the PV-pair rate of 30.3 per triggered event is the number of pairs of **prompt charged particles of the hard-scatter event** (pT > 0.05 GeV, |η| < 1.2) whose mean φ is in the road (`gen_d123.py` l.158-160, `pvpairs()` l.172-179, l.253), on the SM preselected events. N_trig is the sum of those same SM processes. B4 (l.895-899) applies the B1 estimator to the hard-scatter tracks of real no-pile-up Z(νν), W, tt̄ … events, which contain exactly these prompt pairs. v1 added both bounds (D4 l.191, l.197), and v2 adds both floors: 18.5 (PV pairs) + 16.4 (SM) of the 45.3. No overlap rule is given.
- **Why it matters.** This is conservative but not neutral. It inflates the headline floor (§0, [L13]) by up to 18.5 events, and it sends 7.65×10⁷ events of the pool to a parametrised pair gun whose physics is already in the real-event samples.
- **Fixed looks like.** Declare one route for prompt hard-vertex tracks: either B4 only (drop the PV-pair term, or define it as the remainder after B4 attribution), or PV pairs only, with B4 attributing only non-prompt or multi-track hard-scatter candidates. Re-run the allocation and the floor table. If both are kept, show that they are disjoint.

### B3. The quoted extrapolation widths contradict the document's own slope rule (§4.0 pt 7 l.343-345, l.357-360; §4.5 l.793-795; script [12] l.507)
- **Evidence.** "With fewer than three evaluable rungs in the family, σ_b := b₀" (l.344-345). The held-out design tests B1, B2-b and B4-c only at R06-R07 (l.656-658, l.755-756, l.955-956), which is two rungs of the width family R05-R08. So σ_b = b₀ for every estimator except thinning, which has no derivation rungs. The text and script [12] instead use σ_b = 0.029 (pile-up) and 0.097 (single interaction). With the rule as written I get: occupancy model (s_model 0.20, b₀ = ln 1.5) **0.45 / 0.61 / 1.30 / 2.47** at R08 / R09 / R11 / R14, against 0.20 / 0.45 / 1.23 / 2.44 quoted; single-interaction promotion (0.35, ln 2) **0.78 / 1.04 / 2.22**, against 0.36 / 0.78 / 2.11. The R08 widths that will matter most are understated by ×2.2.
- **On the convention itself.** b₀ = ln F per rung, coherent, is anchored only on v1's thinning drift of 0.36 per rung (l.353-355, D4 [U]). That is one estimator, and a failed one. For pile-up, b₀ = 0.405 is barely above it; for F = 2, 0.69 is about twice it. The coherent (linear) form is the conservative one of the two offered (GA-4). It is defensible as a declared convention but not as a measured width, and the paper must say so. "Rung" is not a physical unit (C3).
- **Fixed looks like.** Correct script [12] and l.357-360 / l.794-795. Alternatively, change the rule (for example a slope fitted across R04-R07 with the derivation rungs included, stated as such), and recompute the GA-4 numbers the user will see.

### B4. Log-normal extrapolation widths are not capped at the no-credit value, so a PASS can cost more than NOT TESTED (§4.11 model table l.1112; §4.0 pt 6-7)
- **Evidence.** Estimator outputs (S, π_k, conditionals, the time-tag fraction, the occupancy prediction) enter as log-normal nuisances with width s_tot(r) (l.1112). At s_tot = 2.44 the 84th percentile is ×11.5 and the mean ×19.6 of the nominal (my computation). A probability such as π_k, S or a conditional then exceeds 1 in a large part of the predictive distribution, and the term can exceed its own no-credit value H. Because "leading" and B₈₄ use the predictive distribution (l.1180-1190), a tested-and-PASSED promotion model at d_out ≥ 3 can make a selection *worse* than leaving it NOT TESTED. That is unphysical, and it rewards not testing.
- **Fixed looks like.** Pre-register that every extrapolated factor is truncated at its no-credit value (for example π_k e^θ → min(π_k e^θ, 1), and the term → min(term, H)), or that the term uses whichever of estimate and no-credit value gives the lower B₈₄. Then state the resulting predictive distribution.

### B5. The selection rule is drawn to the edge of the simulation, and "estimate" is defined too weakly to carry the paper's message (§4.11 pt 3 l.1186-1188; §4.12 l.1340-1346)
- **Evidence.** Under Gamma(m+1) a term's predictive mean is (m+1)·κ. Moving one rung deeper, from m = 2 to m = 0, lowers it from 3κ to κ, whatever the physics did. The protections (l.1340-1346) stop the rule from going *past* the first rung at which every leading term is zero, but they do not stop it from *going there*. So T2 is built to prefer bounds over estimates, and the location of the winner is set by M, i.e. by sample size. Second, a term is an "estimate" as soon as every counted factor has m ≥ 1 (l.1186). At m = 1 the width of ln λ is 0.80, against 1.28 at m = 0 (l.1157), so the label does not separate measured from bounded. FRAMING's headline "estimated, not bounded" (l.38-40) could be claimed on single events.
- **Fixed looks like.** (i) Define "estimate" by precision, for example sd(ln λ) ≤ 0.5, i.e. m ≥ 4 by l.1157 (m = 3 gives 0.53). (ii) Report beside the T2 winner the best selection whose leading terms are all estimates, with its G and reach. (iii) Pre-declare in GA-3 which of the two the abstract headlines. (iv) A flat prior per rung ignores λ(r+1) ≤ λ(r) for nested rungs of one sample; at minimum report the winner's sensitivity to a monotone prior.

### B6. The promotion probability π_k treats the missing layers as independent (§4.4 pt 4, l.578-581)
- **Evidence.** π_k is "the product over the k missing layers of the per-layer probability" (l.578-579). Promotion at pile-up 200 is dominated by *correlated* hits: one soft or looping pile-up track, or a pile-up secondary, crosses several adjacent layers inside the same ribbon. That correlation is why the occupancy model of B2 needs "measured layer-to-layer correlation factors" (l.751-752), and why v1 needed its factorisation factor. For k ≥ 2 the independent product is biased low. It is tested only where the closure is evaluable (γ and V⁰ at R06, l.675), not for the k-classes that dominate at deep rungs.
- **Fixed looks like.** Use the occupancy model's correlated multi-layer probabilities for π_k, and show the per-k comparison of predicted and observed promotion on the derivation overlays (l.653-655), split by k.

### B7. The "projected outcome" leaves out the pile-up term at depth and assumes that H counts zero, which has consequences for the paper's claim (§0 l.38-51; [L13] l.1767-1768; GA-3 l.2199-2214)
- **Evidence.** "About 45 events" is the sum of the non-pile-up floors "before any pile-up factor" (Table 4.4a caption l.612-613), and it applies to H only "if H also counts zero" (l.641). Beyond R07 the pile-up term is a one-estimator occupancy extrapolation (l.791-796) with width 0.45-2.5 (B3 above). Its B₈₄ can be comparable to the floor at R09-R11 and dominant at R14+. If the occupancy model fails, nothing beyond R07 is eligible (485-event direct bound, l.779-781). §0 says "with the pile-up term estimated" (GA-3 (i)) but gives no projection of it.
- **What it implies.** The FRAMING message ("estimated, not bounded", FRAMING l.38-40) is, by the strategy's own numbers, **not the expected outcome**. The expected paper is "background bounded at O(40) events by simulation statistics, pile-up extrapolated with a stated width". With the v1-placeholder efficiencies that still gives a reach of about 495-662 GeV (l.45-46, PLAN), well above the existing ≲ 180 GeV limits, so the "beyond existing limits" claim plausibly survives in bounded form. The honest statement in §0 is a strength of v2. The Gate-A sheet should present the bounded paper as the default deliverable, not as a fallback.
- **Fixed looks like.** Add to §0, [L13] and GA-3 a projected pile-up B₅₀/B₈₄ at R09, R11 and R14 from v1's occupancy-type numbers or the development samples (labelled EST), and the floor with H ≥ P_geo stated as an assumption, together with the dependence of the claim on B1 and B2 here.

### C items
- **C1. σ_ln has two definitions.** sd(ln λ) gives 1.28 at m = 0 (l.1157), while "total σ_ln = ½ ln(B₈₄/B₁₆)" (l.1183) gives 1.18 for the same Gamma(1). Use one throughout, or name them differently. TG-1 reads the second.
- **C2. Coverage is the wrong figure of merit for a projection.** There are no observed limits. Exact coverage (I reproduce 0.9448 at κ = 1, λ = 0.5, s = 10; 0.9562 at λ = 3; 0.9816 at κ = 10, λ = 0.5) is automatically conservative when the background is under-estimated, because n is then larger. What matters is the bias of the quoted median expected limit when the true λ is larger (×1.6-4.0 at κ = 35, l.1211-1213, which I reproduce). Pre-declare a criterion on that ratio, or tie the f₀ > 0.5 rule to quoting the 84 % edge as the headline. Holding the auxiliaries fixed in the toys is the right (prior-predictive) choice for a projection. Asimov with an auxiliary of m + 1 (l.1175-1177) over-constrains λ against an observed 0, which matters only for the asymptotic ranking.
- **C3. Unit of extrapolation distance.** The width per *rung* treats a ×2 width step and a fit-probability cut alike. A distance in ln of the extrapolated rejection, ln(P(r_last)/P(r)), anchored with v1's drift per e-fold (about 0.36·3/(5.37 ln 10) ≈ 0.09), would be physically motivated. Offer it in GA-4 as an alternative.
- **C4. Infracolour formula.** The standard vector-current O(α) threshold factor C_F α[π/(2β) − (3+β)/(4π)(π²/2 − 3/4)] gives 3.4 / 6.6 / 13.9 %, against 3.3 / 6.3 / 13.3 % with −4/π (my computation; it has the correct massless limit 3C_Fα/(4π)). The scalar Ẽ needs its own (P-wave) constant. α_IC is more naturally taken at the Coulomb scale m_Q β than at m_Q. All three effects are small, and the band default is conservative.
- **C5. Allocation after the pilot.** Once gains are measured, the optimum becomes M_t ∝ √(N_t/(c_t G_t)), with N_t including the conditionals that will multiply each term at the candidate selections. Pre-register this re-allocation.
- **C6. Categories without time-tag credit.** With no credit both SR-T and SR-G carry the full term (l.161-162, l.640). With a shared λ that is conservative, but in the combination it counts the background twice. State it, or merge the categories when the time tag earns no credit.
- **C7. C1 blinding.** The sealed tier (l.444-461) is a sound design, and the open R04/R06 counts at three points cannot steer the closure verdicts. The remaining leakage channel is the pre-registration's freedom to *remove* T2 candidates (l.1301-1302) after those counts exist. Freeze the full 16-candidate list at Gate A, not only R07 and family F.
- **C8. Proportionality.** In the expected outcome (H and P_geo count zero, B7), survival and promotion buy nothing: they can only lower H, and H is then itself a bound. Of the 914 CPU-h of B1 (l.709-711), the 294 CPU-h of overlays mainly serve tests that Table 4.4b already expects to be NOT TESTED for the leading term. A simpler defensible design spends first on what moves the floor: the biasing pilot, the B2 overlap fix, and the H ceiling test (A1). It makes the full overlay campaign conditional on the pilot showing H ≫ P_geo or on non-zero counts. The R07-direct baseline (l.1317-1319) is a good anchor and should be presented as the robust result.
- **C9. Lepton veto.** Beyond rescaling by an assumed survival (l.1432-1433), give the physics argument. A non-helical trajectory has no tracker track, so it cannot form a particle-flow or global muon, and is at most a standalone muon, which monojet-type vetoes normally do not use. Slow pairs also arrive out of time in the muon system. Retrieve support for this (R-13).

## Overall judgment

**Not yet approved (cycle-1: 11 resolved, 5 partial, 1 deferred acceptably; new: A = 1, B = 7, C = 9).** v2 is a real improvement. The zero-count convention is now coherent, and I reproduce every number of its one-bin table and its coverage. Closures are on disjoint rungs and samples. The expected outcome is stated with unusual honesty. What blocks approval is that the paper's likely claim, "bounded at O(40) events", rests on the ceiling H, and H has no test even though counts exist to test it (A1). Three further defects distort that bound: a PV-pair / SM double count (B2), a pile-up-secondaries test that cannot see its effect (B1), and extrapolation widths that contradict their own rule and are not capped (B3, B4). B5-B7 concern how the selection and the headline are tied to sample size. Each fix is a short change to the text and a few CPU-h of tests already inside the planned samples. None needs new production.
