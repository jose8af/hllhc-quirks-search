## 1. Verdict
- **Yes for 60–80 parameters, not quite for 100.** Best validated configuration (pyhf 0.7.6, **jax backend + scipy
  SLSQP with autodiff gradients, merged channel layout, POI stitched out, fits warm-started from the previous toy**):
  **30 / 168 / 385 / 817 s per unit at 31 / 60 / 80 / 100 parameters** [V], against 483 / 8,814 / — / 67,780 s for the
  numpy+scipy baseline on the same node [V]: speed-up ×16 / ×52 / — / ×83.
- Projected toy programme (STR §4.11 pt 5 + T1-c (iii), 1,006 units): **47 CPU-h at 60, 108 at 80, 228 at 100
  parameters** [E from V units], against 183. Break-even ≈ 93 parameters [E]. Honest uncertainty: a factor ≈ 1.6
  either way (node, load, real workspace structure; §6). The reviewer's 7,600 CPU-h and the stop-and-ask threshold
  (5,992) are no longer in play for the toys.
- Correctness: same q̃ on paired toys (to 1e-6 at 31 parameters; at 100 parameters the recommended configuration
  matches the tight-tolerance minima better than the baseline does), same CLs at μ = 5 on 31 parameters
  (0.1372 ± 0.0165, 1000 + 1000 toys, once ties are handled, §4), **0 failed fits in 25,180 toys** (50,360 fits).
- No change to the statistical procedure was applied. Options that would change it are listed in §7 with their gain.

Profile (fit instrumentation in every job; cProfile jobs `prof-n31`, `prof-n60`) [V]:
- SLSQP needs 15–19 iterations per fit at every size; with numpy each gradient is a finite difference, so a fit costs
  (N+1) × iterations ≈ 470 / 1,020 / 1,900 likelihood evaluations at 31 / 60 / 100 parameters.
- One evaluation of pyhf's likelihood costs 0.26 / 2.1 / ≈ 9 ms: it grows like N³, because pyhf evaluates every
  modifier on the dense (modifier × sample × bin) tensor, and samples ≈ bins ≈ number of counted terms. At 60
  parameters 85–90 % of the CPU is inside pyhf's model (`concatenate` 22 s, `where` 20 s, `einsum` 14 s, `power`
  [normsys code1] 14 s, `make_pdf` 5 s of 90 s); the optimiser itself is negligible. Total ≈ N⁴.
- No failed or pathologically slow fits: max 3,946 evaluations in one fit (100 parameters), 0 failures.
- **Accuracy of the baseline itself** (`p2-c-toy-tune`, 100 parameters, recorded NLL, against a tol = 1e-10 rerun of
  the same toys): with pyhf's default start (λ = m + 1, θ = 0), 90 of 200 free fits stop above the minimum by > 1e-3
  (max 1.30 in twice-NLL) and 4 of 200 fixed-μ fits (max 0.010); 7 toys have |Δq| > 1e-3 (max 0.033). At 31
  parameters jax+scipy leaves 467 of 2,000 free fits up to 0.032 above numpy's minima. These shifts did not move CLs
  at the tested points, but they are the scale against which "same result" is judged below.

Readings:
- (a) and (b2): pyhf's minuit optimiser runs `hesse()` after every valid migrad (pyhf `optimize/opt_minuit.py`), N²
  extra calls per fit; without autodiff it also differentiates numerically. Minuit is slower in every variant. Its
  minima agree with scipy's.
- (b1) gains the factor N of the finite differences (35–45 value+gradient calls per fit at all sizes, against
  470–1,900 evaluations); the N³ cost per evaluation stays (one jax value+gradient ≈ 1.3 numpy values).
- (d) merging the auxiliary Poisson channels into one multi-bin channel (and the two signal regions into one 2-bin
  channel) is the same product of Poisson terms: q̃ identical to 3e-11; gain ×1.1 at 31–60, ×1.06 at 100 (pyhf's
  tensor is still terms × terms). (e1) stitching: identical q̃, ×1.1–1.2.
- (e3) The warm start halves the number of calls per fit (16–19) **and** reaches the true minima: against the
  tol = 1e-10 rerun at 100 parameters it agrees on every q̃ of 200 toys, where the baseline's default start misses by up
  to 0.033. Warm starts are restarted at each ensemble (s+b, b), so a job must run its ensemble sequentially (as it does).
- (c) Fixing negligible rates buys ≤ 6 %: the cost is in pyhf's dense tensor, not in the number of free parameters,
  and the fixed λ change q̃ (by construction). Not useful; T1-f's pruning rule would be needed anyway.
- **Ties in CLs (finding).** With discrete counts many toys have exactly the same data and the same q̃; at μ = 5 the
  observed q̃ (b median 0.60892) is such a value and 3 s+b toys sit on it, so "q̃ ≥ q̃_obs" flips with 1e-7 of
  minimiser noise (raw CLs 0.132–0.137 for configurations whose q̃ agree to 1.4e-6). With q̃ compared to q̃_obs within
  1e-5, every 1000-toy configuration gives 0.1372 exactly [V]. Recommendation for the T1 code (implementation of
  "≥", not a change of statistic): count ties within 1e-5 as "≥".
- Cost against μ: flat (31 parameters jax: 88–90 s at μ = 1.5–6; best config 80 / 100 parameters: 380 / 806 s at
  μ = 6 vs 385 / 817 at μ = 3) [V]. JIT compilation: 1.1–3.4 CPU-s per model and fit type, once per job [V].
  Memory: max RSS 0.44 / 0.54 / 0.66 GB at 60 / 80 / 100 parameters (jax) vs 0.12–0.16 GB (numpy) [V].

## 8. What remains risky
1. Synthetic workspace: the real v2 structure is unknown; re-measure on the first v2 workspace (one 100+100 job, ≈ 3
   min at 100 parameters) before Phase 4b.
2. The env exists only on schrodinger; other nodes need `tools/install/pyhf_toys/install_pyhf_toys_local.sh` (22 s,
   0.8 GB, install slot). jax 0.4.38 with pyhf 0.7.6 worked on all paths used here; pyhf's jax path was not tested for
   the v1-mode workspace (counts 10⁵–10⁶, where T1-b sees fit failures with numpy).
3. Warm starts make q̃ depend (at the 1e-6 level) on toy order; results are reproducible for a fixed seed and order.
4. The baseline's own fits are under-converged at 100 parameters (q̃ off by up to 0.033); whichever configuration is
   used, its accuracy should be stated (the recommended one matches tol = 1e-10).
5. Ties in CLs (§4) can move CLs by ~0.005 at 1000 + 1000 toys unless handled.
6. Node-speed and load factors (§6) are judgements from two nodes and a few loads.
