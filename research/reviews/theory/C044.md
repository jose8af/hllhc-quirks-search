# C044 - VLQ exotic decays to light (pseudo)scalars: theory deep-read (Gate 4)

Reviewer: theory deep-reader (g4t4). Source papers were read from the PDF/text (cache/pdf). Numbers come from the sources unless they are marked UNVERIFIED.

## 1. Claim and framework
- **Mechanism.** In composite-Higgs models with partial compositeness and a gauge-fermion UV completion, the coset that gives the Higgs (SU(4)/Sp(4), SU(5)/SO(5), SU(6)/SO(6), SU(4)^2/SU(4)) always contains extra pNGBs: neutral singlets a/eta/S0, charged phi+ and phi++, and coloured pi6 when QCD is in the coset. These pNGBs can be lighter than the top partners. The top partners couple to them with O(1) strength, because that coupling comes from the same compositeness of q_L that generates m_t. This gives Q -> q + pNGB decays (1907.05894 Sec. 1-2; 2609.00127 Sec. 2.1).
- **Bottom-up version.** 2203.13753 (Eqs. 3-9) adds a singlet or doublet VLQ and an SM-singlet Phi:
  - L ⊃ -lambda^a Phi F̄_L Γ F_R - lambda^b Phi F̄_L Γ q_R
  - VLQ-quark mass mixing mu_F.
- **Why exotic decays are natural.** Standard decays (bW, tZ, th) need the VLQ-quark mixing. Q -> q Phi goes through the off-diagonal lambda and needs no mixing. So for small mixing (theta < 0.05) the exotic branching ratio dominates without tuning (2203.13753 Sec. IV, Fig. 4). Small mixing is also what Z b̄b and FCNC constraints prefer.
- **How the scalar decays:**
  - Elementary singlet: decays at tree level to q q̄ only through mixing, so the tt mode is suppressed by the mixing angles. Below the tt threshold, the loop decay to gg dominates. BR(γγ)/BR(gg) = 9α²Q⁴/(2α_s²), about 0.004 for a T loop and 0.0003 for a B loop (2203.13753 Eq. 42). A singlet cannot decay to WW at the order considered there (Sec. III).
  - Composite pNGB with WZW anomaly couplings: can decay to WW, ZZ, Zγ and γγ. In the photophobic benchmark κ_B = -κ_W, the ratio WW:ZZ:Zγ is 0.65:0.16:0.19 (1907.05894 App. A.2).
  - Charged triplet phi+: BR(Wγ):BR(WZ) = cos²θ_W : sin²θ_W, about 80:20 (1907.05894 Eq. 2.5). This holds **only if its tree-level fermion couplings vanish**. If they do not vanish, phi+ -> tb dominates (Sec. 2.2, scenarios 1-3).

## 2. Controlling parameters and decay chains
- **Parameters:** M_Q, M_S, lambda^{a,b} versus mixing mu_F/M_F (these set BR(Q -> qS)), the scalar decay pattern (tt / gg / bb / γγ / WW), and κ_Φgg (sets direct gg -> S production).
- **Production:** pair production through QCD, which depends only on the mass. Single production is suppressed when mixing is small.
- **Decay chains:**
  - TT -> (tS)(tS), with S -> tt (6t), S -> gg (tt + 4j), or S -> γγ.
  - BB -> (bΦ)(bΦ) -> 6b or 2b4j.
  - X5/3 -> t phi+ -> t W+ γ, or X5/3 -> b̄ pi6 -> b̄ tt.

## 3. Robust vs model-dependent
**Robust:**
- The QCD pair-production cross section (NNLO+NNLL).
- The 6t final state has a high SSL/multilepton rate. Recasts give M_T > 1.3 TeV with 35.9 fb⁻¹ (1907.05894 App. A.1) and M_T > 1.65 TeV at BR = 100% with the full set of recast searches (2609.00127 Sec. 4.1, Fig. 4a).
- The γγ suppression relative to gg is a loop-ratio prediction.
- The 80/20 Wγ/WZ split for a fermiophobic triplet phi+ is fixed by group theory.

**Model-dependent:**
- All branching ratios. With BR(tS0) = 50%, the 6t bound falls from 1.65 to 1.30 TeV (2609.00127).
- Whether phi+ is fermiophobic.
- Whether S has WW couplings (composite only).
- The mass ordering M_S versus 2m_t.
- The degeneracy of several VLQ multiplets. Combining channels gives 1.79-1.86 TeV in the 2609.00127 sample model.

**Bottom line.** The parameter region each target needs survives every constraint I checked:
- Target 1: M_T ≳ 1.6 TeV, BR(tS0) large, M_S0 > 2m_t.
- Target 2: fermiophobic phi+.
- Target 3: S -> gg with M_S < 2m_t.

## 5. Motivation and limiting theory uncertainties
- **Target 1 (6t pair search).** Strongly motivated: a top-philic neutral pNGB is generic in the gauge-fermion composite class (2609.00127 Sec. 4.1 calls it "a generic prediction"). The recast reach of 1.65 TeV with no dedicated search shows the topology has low background. The main limits are the BR dilution and the modelling of SM tttt/ttW/ttZ backgrounds. The measured tttt rate relative to the SM is UNVERIFIED here.
- **Target 2 (SSL + hard photon).** Theoretically narrower, because it needs the fermion couplings of phi+ to vanish for the chosen top-partner representation. 1907.05894 estimates only "10 signal events" contours (Fig. 5). It does not compute a reach, because jet -> γ fakes need data-driven estimates (Sec. 4.1). It is cheap to add, e.g. publishing the leading-photon pT in SSL searches, as the authors recommend.
- **Target 3 (S -> gg).** For an elementary singlet below the tt threshold, gg is the decay you expect by default (2203.13753). It is therefore the rate-dominant channel that the diphoton searches miss. The physics difficulty is tagging gluon two-prong jets against QCD. S -> WW needs composite anomaly couplings and is not generic.

## 6. Distinctiveness
- **6t:** up to 6 b-jets, several same-sign leptons, extreme H_T, very small SM background.
- **SSL + γ:** p_T^γ > 200 GeV with a W-γ system.
- **T -> t(gg) and B -> b(gg/bb):** a double-resonance structure (m_S and m_{qS}) that allows a 2D bump hunt.

## 7. Assessment
- Motivation 4/5. Robustness 3/5 (driven by BR assumptions).
- Status per channel:
  - 6t: searched-limited-regime / indirectly-covered (recast only).
  - Diphoton: well-covered (ATLAS 2607.28381, CMS 2610.03489).
  - gg / WW / Wγ: constrained-but-region-open.
- **Key caveat:** the content of CMS-PAS-B2G-24-005 (INSPIRE recid 3157006, "heavy top partners with charge 5/3 in the single-lepton final state", 2026) was not read. The claim that it targets only X5/3 -> tH+ (tb) is UNVERIFIED.
