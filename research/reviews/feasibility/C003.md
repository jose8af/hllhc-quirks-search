# C003 - Displaced isotropic SUEP: HL-LHC feasibility (Gate 4)

**Target evaluated:** a displaced (dark-meson cτ ~ mm-m), hadronically decaying, isotropic SUEP from ggF/VH mediators, i.e. a high-multiplicity spray of soft displaced tracks and vertices that fails emerging-jet jet requirements and is not muon-rich. The target also includes an HL-LHC projection (Phase-2 L1 track trigger and scouting) of the prompt program.

**Rating: 2/5 (displaced part); about 3/5 for the prompt-program projection. Confidence: medium-low.**

## 1. Production and yields
- 125 GeV Higgs at 14 TeV (HXSWG YR4 14 TeV twiki): ggF 54.61 pb (N3LO), VBF 4.275 pb, WH 1.510 pb, ZH 0.9836 pb, ttH 0.6128 pb. The total is 62.0 pb.
- ESTIMATE (inputs above, L = 3000 fb⁻¹): 1.86×10⁸ Higgs bosons, of which 7.5×10⁶ are WH+ZH. With B(H→SUEP) = 10⁻³ that gives about 1.9×10⁵ SUEP events in total and about 7.5×10³ from VH. These counts are before the leptonic W/Z branching ratio, the trigger and displaced reconstruction. None of those efficiencies has been published for displaced SUEP.
- Present bounds:
  - ATLAS muon-rich prompt SUEP excludes σ×B down to 70 fb at m_S = 125 GeV (B(H→SUEP) of about 0.2%), 0.4 fb at 400 GeV and 0.05 fb at 750 GeV (arXiv:2605.20015, abstract). This is model-specific: it requires N_μ ≥ 5 and a prompt leading muon with d0 significance < 3 (sec. 5). A displaced hadronic SUEP evades it.
  - CMS VH SUEP (arXiv:2604.05996) improves on the ggF result by up to 2 orders of magnitude. It requires electron and muon impact parameters consistent with the PV (sec. 3).
  - CMS ggF offline search (arXiv:2403.05311) defines "prompt" as decay length < 1 mm (p. 2), uses HT > 1200 GeV offline and PV-associated tracks.
  - CMS scouting search (arXiv:2607.09621) lowers HT_HLT from 1 TeV to 410 GeV and improves the limits by up to 10× (pp. 3-5). It keeps only charged PF candidates associated with the PV.
- Heavy-scalar 14 TeV cross sections were not extracted (UNVERIFIED).

## 2. Signature and backgrounds
All existing SUEP searches discriminate using the multiplicity and sphericity of tracks associated with the PV. For displaced dark mesons that handle is gone. 2211.05720 (sec. 3) states: "For signals with displaced tracks, a primary vertex requirement cannot be applied, and high-|d0| tracks from all pile-up vertices must be considered."

Backgrounds for the displaced case:
- material and hadronic-interaction vertices
- K0S and Λ decays
- heavy-flavour decays
- fake or merged large-d0 tracks at PU 140-200
- QCD multijet with ISR (needed for the ggF trigger)

A high count of displaced vertices could make the offline selection nearly background-free, so yields would scale roughly linearly with luminosity. That is plausible but not demonstrated: no study exists, and it is labelled ESTIMATE/qualitative.

## 3. Trigger: the crux
- L1 track threshold: the CMS Phase-2 L1 reconstructs tracks with pT > 2 GeV (2211.05720 sec. 1.1, citing the CMS Phase-2 L1 TDR, INSPIRE 1819968; also 2203.09503 sec. 3.1). For the T = 1 GeV SUEP benchmark, track-multiplicity triggers have "negligible efficiency for all signals" at pT > 2 GeV (2211.05720 sec. 2.2).
- Prompt case at PU200: requiring more than 150 tracks with pT > 1 GeV, or more than 50 with pT > 2 GeV, gives 1-10% efficiency (2211.05720 sec. 3, Fig. 22). These numbers assume PV association, which displaced SUEP loses.
- ATLAS Phase-2 has no hardware track trigger. Tracking runs at the HLT/EF on a 1 MHz input (2211.05720 sec. 1.1).
- Realistic paths for displaced SUEP:
  - VH with single- or di-lepton triggers. These do not depend on the SUEP.
  - ggF via ISR HT or jets, with low efficiency (1612.00850 Table II, L1 efficiencies 3-17% for its benchmarks; 2211.05720 conclusions).
  - L1 displaced-track extensions (1705.04321). Their efficiency for soft multi-vertex sprays is UNVERIFIED.
  - The "belt of fire" hit-counting HLT idea (1612.00850 sec. III.D). For displaced decays its efficiency is expected to drop, because the hits are more diffuse (1612.00850 sec. V, p. 31).
- Phase-2 HLT scouting bandwidth and thresholds: UNVERIFIED (TDR not read).

## 4. Pileup and detector upgrades
- SUEP "strongly resembles pile-up" (1612.00850 sec. I; 2203.09503 sec. 3.1). The original soft-bomb study used ⟨μ⟩ = 50. HL-LHC ⟨μ⟩ up to 200 makes things harder, because the z-localisation of hits is the residual handle.
- What could help:
  - CMS MTD track timing, which could re-associate displaced tracks to a collision in time. This is qualitative; no study was found.
  - Finer Phase-2 tracking.
- What hurts:
  - the 2 GeV L1 track threshold
  - the HLT CPU cost of large-d0 tracking at PU200

## 5. Systematics
In the prompt scouting search the dominant uncertainty is tracking efficiency, up to 50% in the highest-multiplicity bins (2607.09621 p. 5). Large-d0 reconstruction of many overlapping soft vertices would carry larger and less-calibrated uncertainties. Signal modelling of SUEP is also highly uncertain (2203.09503 sec. 3.1).

## 6. Projections and overlaps
- No HL-LHC projection of any SUEP search was found. The HL-LHC Yellow Report BSM chapter (1812.07831) contains none.
- For long lifetimes, a recast of the CMS muon-system shower search gives HL-LHC "soft trigger" limits on dark showers that improve "by up to two orders of magnitude", with peak sensitivity at cτ ≈ 0.3-1.8 m (2505.03058 sec. 5.1, 5.4). ATLAS s-channel emerging jets are most sensitive below 100 mm (as quoted in 2505.03058 sec. 5.1).
- The m-scale part of the target is therefore partly covered or projected. The genuinely open window is cτ of about 1 mm to a few cm with isotropic, non-jet-like topologies.
- Pair-produced/non-resonant SUEP has no benchmark and was not assessed.

## 7. Verdict
- **Biggest obstacle:** displacement removes primary-vertex association, the only demonstrated SUEP-vs-QCD/pileup discriminant at high PU. In addition, the soft products fall below the 2 GeV L1 track threshold. No viable ggF trigger and background strategy exists on paper. VH (lepton-triggered) offline displaced-vertex counting is the realistic route.
- **HL-specific advantage:**
  - The prompt program gains from Phase-2 L1 tracking and scouting.
  - Long lifetimes benefit from the muon-system projection.
  - For the mm-cm displaced isotropic case, any HL-specific benefit (MTD timing, HLT large-d0 tracking) is speculative.
- **Scaling:** the VH displaced analysis could be near background-free, so it would scale roughly with L, but this is unproven. The prompt ggF analysis is background- and tracking-systematics-limited, so it scales slower than L.
