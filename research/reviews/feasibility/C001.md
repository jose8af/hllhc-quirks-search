# Gate 4 Feasibility review: C001 Quirks / quirkonium

Reviewer: Gate-4 feasibility agent g4f3 (independent; read only `candidates/gate4_targets.json` entry C001 plus primary sources).

## Target under evaluation
A dedicated ATLAS/CMS central-detector search for coplanar, non-helical hit patterns from colour-neutral, EW-charged quirks. It targets mQ above the FASER reach (FASER excludes up to 179 GeV at Λ = 10 keV) in the mesoscopic regime Λ ~ 0.1–100 keV. Doing it needs custom (non-helix) Phase-2 tracking and HLT. Secondary targets are a quirkonium resonance plus a soft-track excess, and a Wγ/WZ resonance plus a displaced glueball.

## 1. Current limits (what the HL search must beat)
- FASER, 186 fb⁻¹ at 13.6 TeV, model (1,1,−1) with N_IC = 2. FASER excludes mQ up to 179 GeV at Λ = 10 keV and covers 300 eV < Λ < 100 keV [2607.26195, abstract, p.6, Fig. 5]. Its Fig. 5 also shows the earlier exclusions: reinterpreted CMS HSCP and CMS out-of-time (OoT) limits at low Λ, a reinterpreted ATLAS/CMS monojet limit at high Λ, and DØ. The FASER Fig. 5 axis only extends to ~200 GeV.
- Farina & Low reinterpretation: HSCP limits on uncoloured quirks range from 150 to 650 GeV depending on quantum numbers, in the long-string regime [1703.00912, conclusions]. They identify an "opportunity" in the window 10 cm ≲ ℓ_eff ≲ 10 m [1703.00912, conclusions].
- No ATLAS or CMS dedicated quirk search was found. FASER calls itself "the first LHC search for quirks" and lists ATLAS/CMS strategies only as proposals [2607.26195, refs. 9–15].
- Conclusion: there is a real gap in mesoscopic Λ for EW quirks between roughly 200 GeV and several hundred GeV. Its edges depend on the model. **Status: proposed-not-searched** in ATLAS/CMS.

## 2. Production cross section
- Production is Drell-Yan via γ*/Z, with NLO K-factors of 1.33 at 200 GeV and 1.45 at 1 TeV for the colour-neutral E model [2404.13814, Sec. 2, below Fig. 1]. FASER assigns a 2–4% scale+PDF uncertainty [2607.26195, p.5].
- **ESTIMATE** (no tabulated quirk σ at 14 TeV was found in the text of the sources). I scale from the LHC SUSY xsec WG 13 TeV NLO+NLL right-handed single-generation slepton pair cross sections: 8.15 fb at 200 GeV, 1.68 fb at 300 GeV, 0.50 fb at 400 GeV, 0.18 fb at 500 GeV. These were read via WebFetch from twiki LHCPhysics/SUSYCrossSections13TeVslepslep, which is lossy and should be re-checked. Assumptions:
  - A Dirac fermion with the same EW charges gives ≈4× the scalar rate at β→1, and more near threshold.
  - N_IC = 2 gives a factor of 2.
  - Going from 13 to 14 TeV gives a factor of roughly 1.1–1.3. This factor is UNVERIFIED.
  - Result: σ(pp→QQ̄, mQ = 500 GeV, 14 TeV) ≈ 1.5–2 fb, and ≈ 0.5 fb at 650 GeV.
- Cross-check: Knapen et al. claim discovery (5 events) for EW quirks up to 650 GeV at 300 fb⁻¹ with ε_trig·ε_fid·ε_reco ≈ 0.10 × 0.28 × ~0.7 [1708.02243, abstract and Table I]. That implies σ(650 GeV, 13 TeV) ≈ 0.8 fb. This agrees with the estimate to within a factor of 2.

## 3. Yields (ESTIMATE)
| mQ | σ (14 TeV, est.) | N produced, 3 ab⁻¹ | × ε_trig (0.10) × ε_fid (0.28) × ε_reco (~0.65) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 500 GeV | ~1.5–2 fb | ~4500–6000 | ~80–110 |
| 800 GeV | ~0.15 fb (extrapolated, factor ~2.5–3 per 100 GeV from the slepton table) | ~450 | ~8 |

The efficiencies are the Run-2-like values for the 800 GeV DY benchmark at Λ = 3 keV [1708.02243, Table I]. ε_reco falls to 0.11 at Λ = 1 keV [same table]. ML (GNN) tracking reaches 40–90% reconstruction efficiency for oscillation lengths of 1–10 cm, but the total ε is only ≲1% to ~30% across the plane [2410.00269, Sec. VI, Fig. 9]. If the search stays background-free, the mass reach grows from ~650–700 GeV at 300 fb⁻¹ [1708.02243; 2410.00269 Fig. 9: N > 3 for mQ ≲ 700 GeV] to roughly 850–900 GeV at 3 ab⁻¹. That figure is an ESTIMATE based on the steeply falling DY σ. The lumi gain buys only about +200 GeV in mass and nothing in Λ coverage.

## 4. Backgrounds
- Combinatorial coplanar pile-up hits: ~10⁻³ of events form a 4/8-layer plane and 6×10⁻⁵ form a plane in all layers, at ⟨μ⟩ = 50 [1708.02243, Sec. IV]. Photon conversions in the beam pipe give a fiducial σ of ~10 fb before selection [1708.02243, Sec. III].
- Independent re-analysis [1911.02223, Sec. IV and App. A]: Z(→νν)+jets with pile-up overlaid survives the Knapen selection at **~10⁻² fb, "which cannot be neglected"**. This is ~30 events at 3 ab⁻¹ (ESTIMATE = 10⁻² fb × 3000 fb⁻¹) at μ = 50. At μ = 140–200 the random-coplanar rate should rise faster than linearly in hit density. That rate is not computed in any source (UNVERIFIED).
- The search is therefore **not automatically background-free** at HL-LHC. Extra handles are needed: a full quirk-trajectory χ² fit [1708.02243 Sec. IV; 2410.00269 Fig. 8], dE/dx [1708.02243 App. A], MET–plane alignment, and timing (slow quirks).

## 5. Trigger
- No native trigger exists. Knapen et al. use an ISR-recoil MET trigger with pT(QQ̄) > 200 GeV, giving ε_trig = 0.10 for DY 800 GeV [1708.02243, Sec. III B, Table I]. They warn that MET thresholds "are expected to increase further as the instantaneous luminosity increases" [ibid.]. The L1 muon path is plausible, but the HLT helix match is "likely to fail" and needs a dedicated HLT quirk path, with quirks reaching the muon system within 25 ns [ibid.].
- CMS Phase-2 L1 track trigger: stubs from paired sensor layers reject large-bend tracks corresponding to pT < 2 GeV. Tracklet seeding, projection and Kalman fit assume helices [2110.02826, Sec. 2]. So quirk hits are **not** expected to form L1 tracks. Whether quirk hits form stubs at all has not been studied (UNVERIFIED). L1 TT helps the MET/ISR path through PU-robust track-MET and jets [CMS L1 Phase-2 TDR, INSPIRE 1819968, qualitative].
- HLT: Phase-2 HLT runs full tracking, so a coplanar-plane finder with amortised O(N) complexity "may be sufficiently fast for implementation at the HLT" [1708.02243, Sec. III C]. A GNN on GPUs at the trigger is proposed only in outline [2410.00269, Sec. VII]. No Phase-2 HLT timing study exists.

## 6. Pile-up
- All central-detector sensitivity studies use μ ≤ 50 [1708.02243; 1911.02223] or no pile-up at all [2410.00269: "Effects from pile-up remain to be studied rigorously"; 2509.08878: μ = 1]. **No study exists at μ = 140–200.** This is the dominant unknown.

## 7. Phase-2 detector upgrades
- Help:
  - All-silicon ITk / CMS Phase-2 tracker (CMS: six barrel layers + five endcap disks in the outer tracker [2110.02826 Sec. 2]). More, finer layers mean more hits per quirk plane and better ∆s resolution, and extended η acceptance. Knapen used barrel only, |η| ≲ 1.8 [1708.02243].
  - CMS MTD / ATLAS HGTD (TDRs INSPIRE 1819664, 2091129). Per-track timing can tag slow (β < 1) quirks and reject out-of-time pile-up hits. This is a qualitative inference; no quirk study uses it.
- Hurt: higher hit occupancy, higher MET/jet trigger thresholds, and possibly reduced HLT CPU per event for exotic reconstruction.

## 8. Systematics
In a background-free regime the analysis is statistics-limited. Signal-model uncertainties:
- ISR modelling directly drives the MET-trigger acceptance. FASER's analogous shower-scale variation was ≈6%, up to 28% at small Λ [2607.26195 p.5].
- IC glueball emission (ε = 0.1 per oscillation assumed [2607.26195 p.3]).
- Quirk material interactions and Geant4 modelling.

Background systematics come from extrapolating data-driven random-plane rates to 200 PU. These are UNVERIFIED and likely to dominate if the background is not zero.

## 9. Projection documents and competition
- No ATLAS/CMS HL-LHC projection. HL/HE-LHC YR WG3 (1812.07831) has no quirk projection found by text search (not exhaustively checked).
- FASER2 / FPF at 3 ab⁻¹: discovery up to 1000 GeV for colour-neutral E and 600 GeV for Ẽ, over Λ ∈ [100 eV, 100 keV]. It "significantly surpass[es] those of projected searches at the LHC main detectors" [2404.13814, conclusions]. FPF approval status as of 2026 is UNVERIFIED; 2601.09023 Sec. 2 says FASER2 "is not an approved experiment yet".
- LHCb VELO (Upgrade II, 300 fb⁻¹) projection targets the mid-Λ weak-constraint region with a software trigger [2601.09023, abstract, Sec. 2–4].
- **The central-detector target therefore competes directly with forward/LHCb projections for the same (mQ, Λ) region.** Its added value lies in the larger acceptance for high-pT, central events and in being an approved, running detector.

## 10. Distinguishability
Very distinctive in principle: two quirks lie on one plane with ∆s < 100 µm, non-helical, with anomalous dE/dx along the track [1708.02243, Figs. 3–5]. Once found, the mass and Λ can be measured by a trajectory fit [ibid.].

## Secondary targets (brief)
- Quirkonium dijet resonance plus a soft-track (SUEP-like) excess [2506.11192]: studied only at 140 fb⁻¹, 13 TeV, with no pile-up added. The authors state that "the analysis strategy will have to be revisited for the High-Luminosity LHC with its more challenging pile-up conditions" [2506.11192, Sec. II]. Low-pT track observables are the ones most degraded by PU 200. Track–vertex association with MTD timing could mitigate this, but that is unquantified. Feasibility is low to moderate and unproven.
- Wγ/WZ resonance plus displaced glueball [2504.02940, Sec. 4]: treated as background-free and therefore scales linearly with luminosity. The authors claim it is complementary to present methods "even after the LHC's high-luminosity run" [2504.02940, abstract]. Rate is small (requires ≥5 events; Figs. 8–10). It benefits from Phase-2 LLP triggers, but it is a theory projection only.

## Verdict
- **Single biggest obstacle:** reconstruction and trigger. No non-helical pattern recognition has been validated at μ = 140–200, and the Z→νν+jets+PU coplanar background (~10⁻² fb already at μ = 50 [1911.02223]) makes the "background-free" premise unproven. The trigger relies on an ISR-MET path with ~10% efficiency for DY [1708.02243] and rising thresholds.
- **HL-specific advantage:** if the background stays near zero, 10× luminosity extends EW-quirk mass reach by ~+200 GeV (ESTIMATE). The Phase-2 trackers (more layers, wider η), HLT full tracking, GPU/GNN trigger capability and MTD/HGTD timing all specifically enable the custom reconstruction. None of these exists in Run 3 at the needed level.
- **Feasibility rating: 2/5.** The physics is sound and the signature is striking. But the core experimental prerequisite (custom tracking at PU 200 plus a trigger) is undemonstrated, and FASER2/LHCb projections cover overlapping parameter space.
- **Confidence:** medium.
