# Gate 4 feasibility review: C066, resonant single-LQ via lepton PDFs (e/mu + charm-tagged jet; HL-LHC projection)

Reviewer: HL-LHC feasibility (Gate 4). The only input was the C066 entry in `candidates/gate4_targets.json`. Everything else comes from primary sources.

## Target evaluated
(i) A resonant lepton-quark fusion search, l q -> LQ -> l q, with a charm-tagged jet category (e/mu + c). (ii) Vector-LQ and e-mu LFV interpretations of the lepton+jet resonance. (iii) A collaboration-level HL-LHC projection of the resonant channel.

## 1. Production cross section (sourced)
NLO QCD+QED resonant cross sections at 13 TeV with y = 1 come from 2012.02092 Tables 1-2 (LUXlep-NNPDF31). They are given in pb, for the S1/3 (up-type) or S2/3 (down-type) columns:

| m_LQ | u+mu | s+mu | c+mu | b+mu | PDF unc. on c+mu |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.6 TeV | 1.36e-2 | 1.37e-3 | 7.16e-4 | 4.32e-4 | ±24% |
| 2.5 TeV | 1.50e-3 | 9.53e-5 | 5.43e-5 | 2.39e-5 | ±42% |
| 4.0 TeV | 7.14e-5 | 2.81e-6 | 2.33e-6 | 4.29e-7 | ±63% |

The e-channel numbers are within a few % of the mu-channel ones in the same tables.

- At 2.5 TeV, sigma(c+mu)/sigma(u+mu) = 1/27.6, sigma(c)/sigma(s) = 0.57 and sigma(c)/sigma(b) = 2.3. These are ESTIMATEs computed from the table entries above. The charm channel therefore sits between the s and b benchmarks. This matches CMS 2608.28377 Sec. 2: "Scenarios with couplings to strange or charm quarks would yield intermediate sensitivities, bracketed by these two benchmarks."
- To match the u-quark rate, a c-quark coupling must be about 5.3x larger (sqrt(27.6); ESTIMATE). A minimal scalar has width Gamma = |y|^2 M/(16 pi) (2005.06475 Eq. 2). At y = 3.5, the coupling ATLAS uses for its s/b limits, this gives Gamma/M ≈ 0.24 (ESTIMATE). That is a broad resonance, close to the edge of where a narrow-width bump hunt is valid.
- The 14 TeV cross sections were not found in the sources read. The 13 to 14 TeV gain is UNVERIFIED. It is positive and grows with mass, but I did not compute it.

## 2. Yields before selection (ESTIMATE)
Inputs: sigma(13 TeV) from 2012.02092 Table 1 as a conservative stand-in for 14 TeV, and L = 3000 fb^-1.
- c+mu, M = 2.5 TeV, y = 1: 5.43e-5 pb x 3e6 pb^-1 ≈ 160 events produced. At y = 0.5: ≈ 40.
- c+mu, M = 4 TeV, y = 1: ≈ 7 events produced.
- Applying a c-tag adds a further factor of about 0.3 or less. ATLAS Run-2 DL1 c-tag WP: 30% c-efficiency, light rejection 70, b rejection 9, in ttbar-like pT (2211.16345 abstract). Performance at TeV-scale jet pT is UNVERIFIED and is expected to be worse. The ttbar-regime numbers should not be extrapolated.
- Lepton and jet selection efficiency comes on top of this. In 2005.06475, e ID ≈ 90% at pT > 500 GeV, and mu ID is 69% (57%) at 1 (2.5) TeV.

## 3. Backgrounds
- Dominant: W+jets (1L) and Z+jets (2L). Next: top (b-jet channels) and fake/non-prompt leptons in the e channels (2507.03650 Sec. 6). In 2005.06475 (p. 2), W-+j dominates after the MET cap and jet veto, and the multijet/total background ratio is 28/84/142% at m_ej = 1/3/5 TeV.
- A c-tag on the jet suppresses W+light-jets, the bulk of the background, by the light rejection factor. It keeps W+c and part of W+b (b rejection is only about 9 in 2211.16345). How much this gains in S/sqrt(B) at TeV pT, after paying the about 0.3 signal efficiency, is UNVERIFIED. The gain only matters in the background-limited (low-mass) part of the spectrum.

## 4. Trigger
This is not an obstacle. Run-2/3 analyses use single-lepton triggers. CMS uses a non-isolated single muon with pT > 50 GeV and efficiency > 90% (2608.28377 Sec. 4). ATLAS uses e/mu thresholds of 20-26 GeV isolated and up to 120-140 GeV (e) / 50 GeV (mu) non-isolated (2507.03650 Sec. 3). Signal leptons have pT of about M/2, so they are TeV-scale. Phase-2 L1 single-lepton thresholds are far below this, by an amount I did not verify in a Phase-2 TDR here. Trigger efficiency is therefore expected to be at plateau.

## 5. Pileup
The effect is minor for the TeV-scale lepton and jet. It matters in three places:
- The low-MET requirement (S(pT^miss) cut in 2507.03650; MET < 50 GeV in 2005.06475). Pileup degrades MET resolution.
- The sub-leading-jet veto used in 2005.06475 (pT > 30 GeV). At <PU> = 200, pileup jets would reduce signal efficiency unless pileup-jet ID or timing is used. CMS MTD and ATLAS HGTD target exactly this, but their numbers are not verified here.
- Secondary-vertex-based c-tagging in dense environments. ITk/Phase-2 tracker granularity helps here.

## 6. Phase-2 detector upgrades
- ATLAS ITk + GN2: an ATLAS public note (INSPIRE recid 3130572, 2026) reports that GN2 light-jet rejection with ITk "can be improved by up to a factor 40 in the high-pT" regime relative to the Run-3 tune. The note's abstract was read; the body was not. This is the most relevant HL-specific advantage for heavy-flavour tagging of TeV jets. The abstract quotes b-tagging; the size of any c-tagging gain is UNVERIFIED.
- Extended tracker coverage to |eta| ≈ 4 adds little, because the signal is central (|eta| < 2.5 in 2005.06475).
- Muon momentum resolution at TeV scale limits the mu-channel mass resolution: 6.7%, 12% and 17% at 1, 3 and 5 TeV versus 2.3%, 1.7% and 1.6% for e (2005.06475 p. 3). This favours the e channel for any narrow-peak flavour study.

## 7. Systematics
- The analyses are statistics-limited. ATLAS: systematic impact on mass reach < 5% (2507.03650 Sec. 7). CMS: background-fit statistical uncertainty dominates, and signal systematics are 3-4x smaller (2608.28377 Sec. 8).
- For a c-tagged channel, new systematics enter: c-tag efficiency and mistag scale factors at TeV pT. Calibrations reach 10-20% precision for light and charm in standard pT ranges (ATLAS proceedings, INSPIRE recid 1860177 abstract). TeV-pT calibrations are extrapolated, so the uncertainty there is UNVERIFIED.
- The c+l signal cross section PDF uncertainty is ±24/42/63% at 1.6/2.5/4 TeV (2012.02092 Table 1). This limits the coupling interpretation more than the discovery sensitivity.

## 8. Current limits and projections
- ATLAS 2507.03650, Run 2 + 55 fb^-1 at 13.6 TeV. Channels: e/mu + light-jet (b-veto) and e/mu + b-jet, 1L and 2L. Limits: S̃1 excluded to 3.4 TeV (y_de = 1), 4.3 TeV (y_smu = 3.5), 3.1 TeV (y_be = 3.5) and 2.8 TeV (y_bmu = 3.5) (abstract; Sec. 9). ATLAS states that u/c-coupled models are "kinematically indistinguishable with the studied scenarios, permitting phenomenological re-interpretation through coupling parameter rotation in the quark flavour space" (Sec. 1). The e/mu + c resonance is therefore already indirectly covered: c-jets mostly fall into the b-veto light-jet category, with a small fraction migrating to the b-tag category.
- CMS 2608.28377 (mu only, 138 fb^-1, 13 TeV): 0b/1b x BDT categories. It excludes 1.5-3.6 TeV for lambda_umu = 0.2-0.6, and > 1.8 TeV for lambda_bmu > 1 (abstract).
- Projection: 2005.06475 Figs. 3-4 give a pheno 3 ab^-1 / 14 TeV projection. The ~5.7 (4.2) TeV numbers in the target statement are the masses up to which HL-LHC limits on lambda_eu (lambda_ed) are expected to surpass the Q_W (atomic parity violation) bounds (p. 3). They are not a generic "HL reach". The same paper states s/c couplings are "more difficult to constrain given the suppression of the relevant quark PDFs". No ATLAS/CMS HL-LHC projection of the resonant l+q channel was found. INSPIRE forward citations of 2005.06475 were checked through Aug 2026.
- The review 2505.08738 (Sec. 2 and Sec. 4, conclusions) lists "electron or muon accompanied by a light- or charm-flavored jets" as opportunities. It predates both 2507.03650 and 2608.28377, so the light-jet part of that statement is now covered.

## 9. What scales with luminosity
At high mass, M ≳ 3-4 TeV, background is near zero. There, sensitivity to sigma scales roughly as 1/L until the signal yield runs out: for c+mu at 4 TeV with y = 1, only about 7 events are produced (ESTIMATE). At lower mass the search is background-limited (W+jets), and sensitivity scales as sqrt(L), and only below that if systematics dominate, which they currently do not. The PDF suppression of c and the lepton-PDF suppression together cap the reach. More luminosity extends the c-coupling reach, but because the rate goes as y^2, the coupling reach improves only as L^-1/4 to L^-1/2. That is ESTIMATE-level reasoning.

## 10. Distinguishability
A c-tag cannot distinguish signal from background on its own; that is done by the mass peak. Its value is (a) diagnosing quark flavour after an excess and (b) modest S/B gain at low mass. The e-mu LFV variant (production via e-PDF, decay to mu+q) gives a mu+jet final state already covered by the mu+jet channels, so it is a reinterpretation with BR scaling. Vector LQ: the same final state again, so it is a reinterpretation, but it needs vector-LQ resonant cross sections. The scalar tables of 2012.02092 do not cover them, so their availability is UNVERIFIED.

## Verdict
- **Main obstacle:** the c-quark x lepton-PDF rate is about 28x below u at 2.5 TeV. Matching the u-channel reach needs y_c ≳ 3 (Gamma/M ≈ 0.2), a broad resonance near the perturbative edge. Charm tagging of TeV jets also has low efficiency and is poorly calibrated. Meanwhile the existing b-veto light-jet categories already accept c-jets, so the incremental sensitivity of a dedicated c-tag category is likely small.
- **HL-LHC-specific advantage:** about 20x more luminosity than Run 2 in a channel that is statistics-limited and near background-free at high mass. ITk/Phase-2 tracker flavour tagging, with up to 40x better high-pT light rejection per the ATLAS ITk GN2 note, could make a TeV-scale c/b-tag category usable. The most valuable deliverable is a collaboration-level HL projection of the existing light/b channels, plus a c-coupling reinterpretation.
- **Feasibility rating: 3/5.** The c-tag channel itself is about 2/5 as a physics gain; the HL projection plus reinterpretation is about 4/5. **Confidence: medium.**
